# Content and Voice Guide

## Purpose

Keep the site recognisably Rolina even as subjects change. The identity is not a narrow topic niche; it is the way Rolina approaches things: understand them, improve them and build useful things from what she learns.

## Voice

Aim for: warm, curious, precise, grounded, intelligent without performing intelligence, direct without being cold, and playful when it happens naturally.

Write as someone investigating, building and learning in public — not as an all-knowing authority.

### Good patterns

- State what prompted the thought or project.
- Explain reasoning rather than merely announcing conclusions.
- Distinguish what is known from what is suspected or still being learned.
- Show attempts, decisions, trade-offs and changed opinions when they are relevant.
- Explain technical ideas so a reader can actually understand them.
- Let personality appear without converting every personal interest into a professional lesson.
- Use humour naturally and sparingly.

### Avoid

- Generic thought-leader language.
- Engagement bait.
- “Five lessons X taught me about software” unless there is a genuine, specific connection.
- Manufactured vulnerability.
- Pretending certainty where there is uncertainty.
- Trying to sound clever instead of showing the reasoning.
- Productivity-cult framing.
- Turning every hobby, flower, game or experience into symbolism.

Reminder: **Sometimes you can just really like a bloody daisy.**

## Notes

A Note is something worth recording, explaining, exploring or sharing. It is not a diary entry and does not create an obligation to publish on a schedule.

Notes can include:

- technical explanations and discoveries
- questions being investigated
- lessons from building something
- design observations
- learning notes
- event/conference observations
- thoughtful personal or professional reflections
- science/technology curiosities
- visual notes containing photographs, screenshots, diagrams or video

A Note can be 300 words or 2,000+ words. Length follows the subject.

### Notes should not become

- SEO filler
- a posting calendar obligation
- generic tutorials written only because a blog “needs content”
- recycled social-media posts with no reason to live permanently on the site

## Projects

Project writing should focus on evidence and decisions rather than inflated case-study language. Where appropriate, explain the problem/objective, context, Rolina's role, constraints, what had to be understood, what was built or changed, important decisions, outcome, lessons and possible future improvements.

Do not force every project into exactly the same narrative template. The content body should support an editorial mixture of text, screenshots, galleries, diagrams, code, video and demos.

## Personal content

Personal material can exist because it is interesting, beautiful, funny or genuinely part of Rolina's life. It does not need a professional justification.

The site's personality may include gaming, piano, ballet, wildflowers, machines, bush, science, books, F1, caves, notebooks and other interests as they are genuinely relevant.

## Visual/editorial voice

Prefer generous whitespace, strong modern typography, real images, clear hierarchy and restrained annotations. Use handwritten accents as actual notes, not decorative script everywhere. Motion should be calm, precise and occasionally playful rather than flashy.

## Public posture

Learn. Test. Build. Explain. Change your mind when evidence changes. Show the work.
